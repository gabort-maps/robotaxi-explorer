If all of Cinkota’s buses were electric

226 Cinkota buses · real BKK service, Thu 22 Oct 2026

All buses are taken as electric. Battery levels, charging, grid load and bus changeovers are model results, not measurements. Every bus starts full at 03:00.

Sources, assumptions and limits

Assumptions

Buses and batteries

  • All 226 buses are electric. The timetable is real BKK service on Thu 22 Oct 2026. The day repeats, and every bus starts full at 03:00.
  • 22 Oct was the last day before the autumn break and ran the Friday timetable.
  • Distance driven comes from one position a minute plus 9 %. Buses that leave the recorded area are understated.
  • Usable battery, today: 450 kWh (18 m), 375 kWh (12 m), 190 kWh (midi). The midi figure has no verified source.
  • Usable battery, 2028–29: 560 kWh (18 m), 450 kWh (12 m), 225 kWh (midi), and consumption 5 % lower. These are our assumptions. BKK’s draft tender asks for at least 500 / 400 / 200 kWh net, and one supplier states 633 / 475 kWh usable as its LFP maximum (articulated / solo), so our values sit between BKK’s minimum and the top of the market.
  • The batteries are new. Ageing is not modelled.
  • 10 % of the usable battery must be left at the end of the day (the reserve).

Consumption, heating and cooling

  • Consumption per km is a fixed value per bus type: 1.35 / 1.00 / 0.80 kWh/km in October and 1.97 / 1.46 / 1.17 kWh/km on the cold day. These are our estimates between published fleet figures from Hamburg and Hungary. They are not Budapest measurements.
  • The cold day is -5 °C for the whole day, with a heat pump. The heat pump adds 46 % to consumption. BKK's draft bus specification designs the heating for -10 °C with a separate passenger heater, and does not say how that heater is powered.
  • The buses are pre-heated from the grid while plugged in, so pre-heating takes nothing from the battery.
  • Cooling and hot days are not modelled. Passenger load, hills and driving style are not modelled either.

Charging

  • Garage: 150 kW at the plug, 93 % efficiency, so 139.5 kW into the battery. Every visit loses 5 minutes at plug-in. The same power is used for all bus types.
  • Charging sites: 300 kW at the plug, 93 % efficiency, from minute 3 of a stop, on at most the bus's 3 longest waits at open sites.
  • Power is flat from empty to full. There is no slowdown near full, no cold-battery slowdown and no power sharing between buses.

Garage and grid

  • Managed charging is an optimiser with full knowledge of every bus. It keeps the garage load within BKK's planned grid limit and uses that limit as evenly as possible through the day. It is a best case. Unmanaged means every bus plugs in on arrival.
  • Grid need = (peak MW x 1.25 + 3 kW per bus) / 0.95. The 1.25 margin and the 3 kW base load are our choices.
  • Grid limit at Cinkota, from BKK's draft infrastructure specification (July 2026): 10 MVA from 22:00 to 06:00 and 5 MVA from 06:00 to 22:00. BKK requested 3 x 5 MVA, of which one 5 MVA unit is a reserve. We take the whole limit as available for charging, at a power factor of 0.95: 9.5 MW at night and 4.75 MW by day.
  • If the buildings and a 25 % margin come off the limit first, charging only what the next day needs still fits on the cold day. Refilling all 226 buses to full every cold night would then go over by 7 to 13 %.

Bus changeovers

  • A full bus takes over at an end station. The handover takes 10 minutes, and the replacing bus leaves with at least 15 % battery.
  • The taken-off bus drives back empty and charges to full before it leaves again. Empty drives use the road distance and the same kWh/km as the bus type.
  • 16 spare buses are treated as electric buses that start full. Today most of them are diesel or hybrid.
  • The 16 spare buses are buses that ran no timetable trips on 22 Oct. Whether they are all available is not known. They are treated as available and full, which is the best case.
  • Empty drives use routed road distance and car speed, with no traffic. Changeover margins of a few minutes are tight.
  • Replacing buses do not charge at the sites on the extra duty. All other buses keep their original site times.

Limits of this model

  • These are model results, not measurements. There is no measured Budapest e-bus data behind the consumption or the charging.
  • Read the results as orders of magnitude. Peak load and energy are good to about plus or minus 15 %, mostly because of the consumption assumption. Charging times are somewhat optimistic because of the flat power.
  • The conclusions are robust: managed charging keeps the garage within BKK's planned grid limit, while unmanaged charging goes far over it; changeovers lower the garage load; cold weather is far harder than October; and battery range, not the grid connection, is the limit.
  • The counts are not robust. How many buses drop under the reserve, and how many changeovers are needed, are threshold counts. A small change in consumption can move them by tens of buses.
  • Margins of a few minutes or a few percentage points are inside the model's error. Read them as "tight", not as "passes" or "fails".
  • The model covers one weekday, one cold day and one garage, with a repeating schedule.
  • The grid load covers charging only. It does not include preconditioning, battery preheating, the backup storage or the buildings. BKK’s specification requires the first two, so the real load on the connection will be higher than shown.
  • It is not a charging design, a grid application or an operating plan. A real design needs measured consumption, the real chargers and a detailed grid study.
  • Independent analysis; not produced, reviewed or endorsed by BKK.

Sources and methods

Positions
BKK FUTÁR bus positions recorded about once a minute on Thu 22 Oct 2026, within 20 km of the centre. A bus outside that circle, or without signal, has gaps.
Silent gaps (over 3 minutes)
In a garage or at Örs vezér tere the bus stays where it was last seen, drawn faint. At the 20 km edge it is moved along the route shape of its line, drawn as estimated. If a bus reappears more than 300 m away, it waits and drives the last part in a straight line, also drawn as estimated.
Battery model
Usable 450 / 375 / 190 kWh (18 m / 12 m / midi). October 1.35 / 1.00 / 0.80 kWh/km, cold day 1.97 / 1.46 / 1.17. Strict garage rule: a bus charges at a garage only at its own night garage, Cinkotai autóbuszgarázs (150 kW x 93 % from minute 6, between its first and last trip). Every other place where a bus stands 12 minutes or more (end stations, other BKK garages, storage sites, inferred sites) is a possible charging site: 300 kW x 93 % from minute 3, at most on the bus’s 3 longest waits at open sites. Sites are opened one at a time, best first (the ladder): the site that makes the most extra bus-days work on the cold day goes first, until no site helps. Today’s batteries open 13 sites, the 2028–29 batteries 9. 2028–29 set: 560 / 450 / 225 kWh, consumption × 0.95. Power in the Now boxes is grid side: the buses drawing power at that minute × 150 kW at the plug. “Dropped under the 10 % reserve” counts buses whose battery is under 10 % of usable at some minute of the day.

Distance and time statistics
Km so far is the model’s road km (straight line x 1.09). The median bar is the median km of all buses at the clock time; each stretch takes the median battery of the buses that have reached that km. The five buses with the most km so far are ranked afresh every minute. The pie counts the whole day from 03:00 to the clock time (24 h at the end), including off duty: parked before the first departure and after the last arrival. Slices: moving in service; empty run (driving out of service, shown by km growing); short stop under 30 min; stop of 30 min or more; garage stop. Standing out of service counts as a stop, by the length of the run. Silent time the model has as off the road is counted as the map draws it: in the garage, standing, or moving (as an empty run). On the top 5 bars a dot marks a charge (blue Cinkota garage plug, magenta charging site), a hollow dot a stop of 30 min or more that got no charge, and a red cross the minute the model battery reaches 0 (the model keeps the bus running). With changeovers on, a light violet diamond marks where a weak bus was handed over and a violet triangle where a replacing bus took over.
Charging and the night
From the energy model. Connected: a bus plugged in at the Cinkota garage for the whole stop, and for the whole night from getting home to leaving. Drawing power: from 5 minutes after the start, 139.5 kW into the battery (150 kW at the plug, 93 %), until the kWh of the stop are taken; at night until the battery is full. The night battery levels on the map and in the bars follow that schedule with the full refill, so all buses except the few whose night is too short to refill are full in the morning (the Garage and grid tab gives the number). The night follows the schedule also after 03:00, so a bus that comes home late is not yet full then. The day repeats, so buses arriving after 03:00 are shown full until they arrive. Nine buses have no night: six with a duty longer than 24 h and three with 6 h or more without data inside the duty. The day levels are the replay’s. Power from the grid in the Now boxes is the buses drawing power at that minute × 150 kW at the plug (grid side); the charts show the energy model’s series (15 minute mean, grid side). Never the connected buses × 150 kW. Charging sites: the buses connected there are counted, their own load is not in the energy files. Model result, not a measurement.
Changeovers
The changeover plan for this day: the changeover point and the replacing bus are chosen so that the most failing buses are saved, then the least empty driving, then the least driver time. A bus is failing if its battery goes under 10 % at any minute. A changeover happens at an end station within 11 km by road of the garage (11 km is a calculation basis, not a rule) and only before the failing bus first goes under 10 %. The replacing bus (drive out included) and the bus taken off (drive back included) must stay at 10 % or more, and the bus taken off must be full again before it leaves again, with the charging time worked out from the energy needed. With changeovers on, the garage numbers are the energy model’s recompute with the changeovers; on the road the replacing bus’s battery follows what the failing bus would have used, scaled to the plan’s level at the end of the duty. With charging sites the sites are used first and the changeovers only take the buses that still fail with them. The empty drives and the 10 minute handover count as empty run.
Daylight (visual only)
The map goes dark after sunset. The sun height is computed with the standard NOAA solar formulas for central Budapest (47.50 N, 19.04 E): 22 Oct 2026 (UTC+2) for the October case, 21 Dec 2026 (UTC+1) for the cold day. The map is fully light with the sun about 3 degrees above the horizon and fully dark at 9 degrees below it, with a smooth fade in between. The bus glow is only a drawing effect. No BKK or battery value depends on it.
Garage synergies
Real BKK bus positions about once a minute on Thu 22 Oct 2026, within 20 km of the centre, for the seven garages Cinkota, Bogáncs, Óbuda, Kelenföld, Andor, Dél-pest and Szállító; a bus belongs to its night garage (where it stands after its last trip). Stands of 12, 20 or 30 minutes and more count (30 is the main result); garages’ own depots and storage sites are left out. Shared time at a place is the time buses of two garages stand there together. Pair hours add the pairs, so a place where three garages meet counts for each pair; the totals over the map count each place once. The order of joining adds, each time, the garage that shares the most time with the garages already in the group: an order, not the best group. This view is a screen of where garages stand together. It does not say the buses are the same size, free or charged, or that a place has a grid connection or free stands, and it is not a changeover or charging plan. About these numbers

With charging sites the garage load is small because most charging moves to the sites. It does not mean the fleet needs almost nothing: the charging moves, it does not disappear. The purple line is the load of the whole Cinkota fleet at the end stations and other places (grid side, 15 minute mean, 300 kW per bus, no power sharing, the buses charge as soon as they wait at an open site). It is not the connection for one site. With changeovers on, the weak buses that were handed over stop using the sites after their changeover (1 Nov 2026); the replacing buses do not charge at the sites on the extra duty, as in the changeover plan.

The 95 % rule is a clause of BKK’s draft infrastructure specification (July 2026): at least 95 % of the buses of a depot must be able to charge at the same time. The 41 MW marker is our reading of that clause at BKK’s minimum charger powers (70 kW midi, 150 kW solo, 250 kW articulated): 95 % of the 226 buses, 41.4 MW. It is our reading, not a confirmed requirement. The faint line on the time charts is the shape of Hungary’s national demand in 2025 (Electricity Maps; 3.0 GW at 11:00 and 5.5 GW at 18:00, the energy model’s figures): only those two points, smooth between them, relative and not to scale, drawn with no values; the band is the evening ramp, 17:00 to 21:00. It is not Budapest 2026 data. Cold day: −5 °C with a heat pump, our assumption; BKK gives no winter assumption. Power is from the grid; energy per bus is into the battery.

    Replay build v47 · 1 Nov 2026

    All 226 Cinkota buses can run electric, if the operation flexes when the weather turns.

    Cold day (-5 °C) · today’s batteries · full night refill · click a picture to open it in the tool

    1It can work with today's technology

    GARAGE ONLY OR BUS CHANGEOVER OR CHARGING AT THE STANDS
    Service km: delivered failed (driven under the 10 % reserve)

    Cold day, garage charging only: 133 of 226 buses fail, yet 81 % of service km is delivered. With bus changeovers, 21 fail (97 % delivered). With charging where buses already stand, 5 fail (over 99 % delivered), and none with the next battery generation.

    2Two ways to close the gap, and they combine

    Pay upfront so the operation never changes, or build flexibility into the operation. The question is how much to buy upfront, not whether.

    3Plan for the normal day, flex for the hard one

    The operation is built around the timetable, and that stays, but it also has to flex with the weather. The same timetable leaves 49 buses short in October and 133 on a cold day. Changeovers, stand charging and managed charging are needed only in the weeks that call for them: a cold week, or to a lesser degree a heatwave.

    4The room is in the operation

    A bus moves in service only a third of the day. For 40 % of the day it stands still for 30 minutes or more: at the garage, before and after its shift, and at long stops. Each is a chance to charge. The median duty is 253 km and the longest 427 km. Only 5 are over 400 km, so rebalancing them is a small step.

    5The garage is the wrong unit

    BUS CHANGEOVERS SHARED STANDS

    With 16 spare buses, changeovers at end stations rescue 112 of the 133 failing duties (spares assumed available and fully charged). The map shows where buses of different garages already stand together, so stands and spares could be shared.

    6Next step

    DDepot by depot

    Repeat the exercise on every garage and the whole Volán fleet, measure a real winter, and roll out depot by depot to learn, not to fix the design.
